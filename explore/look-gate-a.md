# Look gate A, sanity, round 1

Reviewed `explore/shots/a-desktop.png` at 1440×900 and `explore/shots/a-mobile.png` at 390×844, then the launch heroes of Antipoda, Plinks, Laureate, Nightcap, Nullius, Yesca, Traza, Maqueta, and Mellizo.

Sanity fails the gate. First impression is 7. The other two axes pass at 8.

The desktop still is a finished cracktro frame. Copper bars sit in even 86px side margins. The name MIGUEL is a white-to-red metal logo on a sine, with a ghost offset behind the stems. Under it, a smaller line reads ANOTHER GREETS DEV PRODUCTION. A scroller along the bottom is caught mid-pass on HAIL MIGUEL and 100% PROC, cut at the raster edge the way a scroller should be cut. Stars are several sizes and several grays. GREETS sits at the lower left. The lower right joins greets.dev/miguel to the words press any key to boot. A scanline stripe is in the pixels, one darker row in every three, about a tenth darker on the flat black. The bezel, the glow, and the corner radius leave no trace. The raster is the page, which is what this direction asked for.

The phone still is the same raster with the sides thrown away. The copper runs off both edges of the glass. MIGUEL still fits, with about 20px of letter margin, because six letters happen to sit in the middle of the crop. Stars in that band reach within a few pixels of the glass. Below the tube there is a tall empty band, then a centered stack. GREETS is bright. The hint wraps, and the word boot sits alone on the next line.

## First impression: 7

On the desktop shot this reads as a product in one look. The palette is red, white, and near-black. The type inside the raster is blocky and specific. The outside labels stay out of the picture.

It stays under 8 because the two frames do not agree, and because the page contradicts its own instruction.

The phone shows a center slice of a wider frame, plus a large dead band under the slice. The full 320×200 frame, with bars above and below, is the desktop arrangement. A six-letter name survives. The product is a URL for any name, and this crop has no safe area left for a longer one. The wrapped hint is a layout bug you can see without guessing at other names.

The boot line is also doing the wrong job on both frames. The tube is already on. The logo is already formed, the bars are already drawn, the scroller is already moving. The only sentence on the page says to press a key to boot. That reads as a caption pasted under a screenshot of the result. The hint is the faintest type on the screen, about rgb 85, 85, 110 on rgb 2, 2, 4, contrast near 2.9 to 1. GREETS beside it is near 17 to 1. The instruction the page depends on is the text a visitor is least likely to read. On a phone, "press any key" names a control the device lacks.

The desktop frame is distinctive. Taken together, the two frames still look unfinished.

## Signature moment: 8

The moment in the brief is a keypress that boots the tube, then the visitor's own name rippling into the logo as the tune comes in.

The ripple is already in the still. Letter tops step along a sine, the red runs out of the bottom of the stems, and a dim copy of the letters sits offset from the bright one. That is a specific picture, and the word is this visitor's name. A still leaves the tune out. The picture the tune would land on is already the right one.

The boot has no frame of its own. The stills show the logo already up, the glass already lit, the tube already past the wait. The keypress exists as 8px type under a demo that has already played its hit. On the phone the same line is both unreadable as an action and impossible to perform. So the potential is real, and the composition has not yet made a place for the before. That caps it at 8.

## Originality: 8

Against stock retro web, the risky moves are the ones this direction is built from, and most of them come out specific.

The scanline overlay is the stock one. It is visible if you sample a flat row, and it stays a texture. The starfield, frozen, is also a stock ground. The copper and the logo are what make the frame a cracktro. The bars are red, white, and black bands of uneven weight, top and bottom, in the shape of a copperlist. The logo is a per-column wave with a trail. The scroller copy, HAIL MIGUEL and 100% PROC, is scene language. A stranger may not know what 100% PROC means. They will still see that the sentence is generated for the name on the URL.

Against the nine shipped heroes, a glance keeps this separate. The studio habit that remains is the diagram under the picture. One object, centered, on a quiet field, with a short line of instruction in the margin. Antipoda, Yesca, Laureate, and Nightcap all use a version of that diagram. Sanity uses it too. The object inside the diagram is new. No shipped product has a metal pixel name, copper bars, or a scroller.

That is an 8. It would fall if the raster were a green terminal or a scanline filter over ordinary type. It would rise if the phone frame and the outside labels stopped repeating the studio's "one window, caption underneath" poster.

## What is already holding

The desktop raster is the whole argument for this direction, and it holds. Even margins, a logo that is obviously a name, copper that reads as copper, a scroller that reads as a scroller, outside type that stays in the margin. The scanlines stay quiet enough to remain a texture. The nearest studio neighbor is the dark poster with one window.

## What is not finished

The phone crop. The raster should meet the glass as a whole 320×200 frame, with bars above and below if the phone is tall. The current shot cuts the sides and then leaves a dead band under the tube.

The boot line. It describes a state these stills never show, it fails contrast at about 2.9 to 1, and on the phone it wraps and asks for a key the phone lacks. Either the first frame shows the tube off, with a control a phone can actually hit, or the running frame stops asking to be booted.

The safe area for the name. MIGUEL fits the phone slice. The slice is the bug. The logo is the product, so the longest name the URL accepts has to land inside the glass on both frames, including the trail.
